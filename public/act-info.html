What is ACT?

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy — a mindfulness-based approach focused on accepting difficult thoughts and feelings rather than fighting them.

The basic idea

ACT works from a different angle than trying to eliminate unwanted thoughts or feelings. Instead, it helps you make room for them — accepting that discomfort is part of life — while committing to actions that line up with what genuinely matters to you.

A core idea is "cognitive defusion": learning to notice a thought as just a thought, rather than a fact you have to obey or fight.

What a course usually looks like

Sessions typically explore what you value in different areas of life (relationships, work, health), practice mindfulness and acceptance exercises, and build concrete steps toward value-aligned action even when difficult feelings are present. Courses often run 8–16 sessions.

What it's commonly used for

ACT has a solid evidence base for anxiety, depression, chronic pain, OCD, and workplace stress — particularly where avoidance or struggling against thoughts and feelings is keeping someone stuck.

What to expect from an initial assessment

An initial session usually explores what matters most to you, patterns of avoidance you've noticed, and how open you are to mindfulness-based techniques as part of treatment.
