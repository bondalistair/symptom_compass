What is Psychodynamic Therapy?

Psychodynamic Therapy — an exploratory approach that looks at how past experiences and unconscious patterns shape present feelings and relationships.

The basic idea

Psychodynamic therapy works from the idea that patterns formed earlier in life — often in close relationships — continue to shape how we feel, relate to others, and react to situations today, sometimes outside our conscious awareness. The therapy aims to bring those patterns into view so they can be understood and changed.

What a course usually looks like

Sessions are typically less structured than CBT or DBT — more free-flowing conversation than set exercises or homework — and can range from shorter-term work (12–24 sessions) focused on a specific issue, to longer, open-ended therapy for deeper personality-level patterns.

What it's commonly used for

It's often used for recurring relationship difficulties, longstanding low mood, a sense of being stuck in repeating patterns, and difficulty understanding your own reactions — areas where more structured, skills-based approaches haven't been the full answer.

What to expect from an initial assessment

An initial conversation usually explores your history, relationship patterns, and what's brought you to therapy now, and helps both of you get a sense of whether this more exploratory style feels like a good fit.
