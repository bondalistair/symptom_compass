What is DBT?

Dialectical Behavior Therapy — a skills-based therapy that balances acceptance with active change, originally developed for intense emotional swings.

The basic idea

DBT was originally developed for people experiencing chronic suicidality and intense emotional instability, and centers on a "dialectic": holding acceptance of where you are right now alongside a commitment to change. It teaches concrete, practical skills rather than relying solely on insight or conversation.

What a course usually looks like

DBT usually combines individual therapy with a weekly skills group covering four areas: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness. It's typically longer-term — often six months to a year — and some programs include phone coaching for real-time support during a crisis.

What it's commonly used for

DBT has the strongest evidence for chronic emotional dysregulation, self-harm, intense mood swings, unstable relationships, and borderline personality patterns. It's also used alongside other treatment for some eating disorders and substance use.

What to expect from an initial assessment

An assessment usually covers current safety, the intensity and frequency of emotional swings, and whether the combined individual-plus-group format is realistic for your schedule and needs.
