What is IPT?

Interpersonal Therapy — a focused therapy that treats current relationships and life transitions as the key drivers of low mood.

The basic idea

IPT works from the idea that mood problems are closely tied to what's happening in your relationships and life right now — grief, conflict with someone close to you, a major role change (like becoming a parent or losing a job), or persistent isolation. Rather than exploring the distant past, it works directly on improving one or two of these current problem areas.

What a course usually looks like

IPT is time-limited and structured, typically running 12–16 sessions built around a specific focus area identified early on, with concrete goals for improving communication or navigating a transition.

What it's commonly used for

It's most established for depression — particularly when it's connected to a clear life transition, loss, or relationship conflict — and has strong evidence for postpartum depression specifically.

What to expect from an initial assessment

An initial session usually maps out your current relationships and any recent life changes, to identify which single area the therapy will focus on.
