What is Family Therapy?

Family / Systemic Therapy — an approach that treats difficulties as part of a wider relationship system, not just something happening in one person.

The basic idea

Rather than treating one person in isolation, family or systemic therapy looks at the wider network of relationships — a family, a couple — and works on the communication patterns, roles, and dynamics between people that may be contributing to a problem.

What a course usually looks like

Sessions often involve multiple family members together rather than one individual alone. Some approaches are fairly short-term and focused on specific patterns; others run longer, especially where dynamics are more entrenched.

What it's commonly used for

It's commonly used for family conflict, adolescent behavioral difficulties, couple and relationship distress, and — particularly for younger people — as part of family-based treatment for eating disorders.

What to expect from an initial assessment

An initial session usually involves meeting with the relevant family members together, to understand the dynamics at play and agree on what everyone hopes will be different.
