What is Exposure Therapy?

Exposure Therapy — a gradual, structured approach to facing feared situations so avoidance and anxiety can ease over time.

The basic idea

Exposure therapy is built on a well-established pattern: avoiding something we fear keeps the fear alive, while gradually and safely facing it, in a controlled way, lets anxiety naturally decrease over repeated exposure. It's often used as a specific technique within a broader course of CBT.

What a course usually looks like

You and your therapist build a "hierarchy" of feared situations, from mildly uncomfortable to most distressing, and work through them step by step — starting with easier ones and building confidence before progressing, always at a pace you agree to.

What it's commonly used for

Exposure therapy has strong evidence for specific phobias, OCD, panic disorder, PTSD, and social anxiety — conditions where avoidance is a central part of what keeps the problem going.

What to expect from an initial assessment

An initial session talks through exactly what you avoid and why, and starts sketching out a hierarchy of situations to work through together, always at a pace that feels manageable.
